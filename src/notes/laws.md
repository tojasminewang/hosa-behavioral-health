## Federal laws related to SAMHSA
@ [SAMHSA: Laws, Regulations, and Policies](https://www.samhsa.gov/about/laws-regulations-policies) · [SAMHSA FAQs](https://www.samhsa.gov/about/faqs)

| Law | Year / Public Law | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (ACA) | 2010 · PL 111-148 | Included several provisions aimed at helping people living with mental health conditions or substance use disorders |
| Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) | 1990 · PL 101-336 (amended 2008, PL 110-325) | Equal opportunities in employment, state and local government services, public accommodations, commercial facilities, transportation, and telecommunications for citizens with disabilities, including people with mental illnesses and addictions |
| Bipartisan Safer Communities Act (BSCA) | 2022 · PL 117-159 | Gave SAMHSA unprecedented supplemental funding to address the nation's mental health and substance use crises and make communities safer |
| Comprehensive Addiction and Recovery Act (CARA) | 2016 · PL 114-198 | Authorized over $181 million each year to respond to the opioid epidemic, increasing prevention programs and the availability of treatment |
| Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act (MHPAEA) | 2008 · PL 110-343 | Mental health and substance use benefits must be comparable to general medical coverage |
| STOP Act | 2006 · PL 109-422 | Authorized a grant program to prevent and reduce alcohol use among youth ages 12–20 |
| SUPPORT Act | PL 115-271 | Addressed the opioid overdose epidemic; reauthorized in December 2025 |
| Tribal Law and Order Act (TLOA) | 2010 · PL 111-211 | Consistent federal focus on issues in Indian country, including alcohol and substance use prevention and treatment |
| 21st Century Cures Act | December 2016 | Reauthorized SAMHSA and established the Assistant Secretary for Mental Health and Substance Use |

- **Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act of 2008 (MHPAEA):** Requires insurance groups offering coverage for mental health or substance use disorders to make these benefits comparable to general medical coverage
  - Deductibles, copays, out-of-pocket maximums, and treatment limitations for mental health or substance use disorders must be no more restrictive than those for other medical care
  - Full name: the Paul Wellstone and Pete Domenici Mental Health Parity and Addiction Equity Act of 2008
- **CARA (2016):** Authorized over $181 million each year (it must be appropriated each year) to respond to the opioid epidemic
- **STOP Act (2006):** Authorized a grant program to prevent and reduce alcohol use among youth ages 12–20 for current or former grantees under the Drug Free Communities Act of 1997
  - Listed with it: SAMHSA's "Talk. They Hear You." campaign (helps parents, caregivers, educators, and communities prevent underage drinking and other substance use) and the federal Interagency Coordinating Committee on the Prevention of Underage Drinking
- **SUPPORT Act:** The Substance Use-Disorder Prevention that Promotes Opioid Recovery and Treatment for Patients and Communities Act (PL 115-271); strengthens the behavioral health workforce by increasing addiction medicine education, expands access to evidence-based care and trauma-informed care, and increases prevention efforts
- **Tribal Law and Order Act of 2010:** Requires interagency coordination among the Department of Justice (DOJ), the Department of the Interior (DOI), and HHS
- **ADAMHA Reorganization Act of 1992:** The Alcohol, Drug Abuse and Mental Health Administration Reorganization Act (PL 102-321), enacted July 10, 1992; amended the Public Health Service (PHS) Act to establish the MHBG and SUBG block grants
- The block grant law was later amended by the **Children's Health Act of 2000** (PL 106-310)
- **HIPAA:** The Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (PL 104-191, August 21, 1996); privacy standards for individually identifiable health information are in 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164
- **Controlled Substances Act:** Federal drug policy regulating the manufacture, importation, possession, use, and distribution of controlled substances; some medications for SUD are controlled substances
- **Federal Drug-Free Workplace Program:** Mandated by Executive Order 12564 and Public Law 100-71; addresses illegal drug use by federal employees, certifies executive agency drug-free workplace plans, and identifies safety-sensitive positions subject to random drug testing
- SAMHSA's Center for Substance Abuse Prevention (CSAP) oversees HHS-certified laboratories that follow the mandatory guidelines for federal workplace drug testing

## Federal regulations (Code of Federal Regulations)
- Many federal regulations related to SAMHSA are in Title 42 (Public Health) of the Code of Federal Regulations (CFR)

| Regulation | What it covers |
|---|---|
| 42 CFR Part 2 | Confidentiality of substance use disorder (SUD) patient records ("Part 2 records") |
| 42 CFR Part 8 | Certification of opioid treatment programs (OTPs) |
| 42 CFR Part 51 | Protection and advocacy programs under the PAIMI Act of 1986 |
| 42 CFR Part 51d | Grant applications by states and Indian tribes for community emergency response related to substance use and mental illness |
| 42 CFR Part 54 / 54a | Charitable Choice regulations (block grant and homelessness formula grants / discretionary funding) |
| 45 CFR Part 96 | Regulations for the SUBG block grant (published March 31, 1993) |
| 45 CFR Parts 160 and 164 | Standards for Privacy of Individually Identifiable Health Information (HIPAA privacy) |

- **42 CFR Part 2:** A federal law (42 U.S.C. 290dd-2 and 42 CFR part 2) that protects the confidentiality of patient records for people receiving services for substance use disorders
  - Part 2 applies to any federally assisted program that provides SUD diagnosis, treatment, or referral for treatment; these are called Part 2 programs
  - Many practitioners in general medical facilities do not meet the definition, so Part 2 generally does not apply to their records even if they prescribe buprenorphine; HIPAA protections still apply
- **42 CFR Part 8:** Governs treatment of opioid dependence with opioid medications through an accreditation and certification-based system for opioid treatment programs; acknowledges that addiction is a medical disorder that may require differing treatment protocols for different patients
  - The Part 8 regulations went into effect in 2001; the revised final rule was published February 2, 2024, went into effect April 2, 2024, and had a compliance date of October 2, 2024
  - The 2024 rule made COVID-19 Public Health Emergency flexibilities permanent, removed stigmatizing or outdated language, promoted practitioner autonomy, and removed all DATA Waiver language
  - SAMHSA's **Division of Pharmacologic Therapies** (part of CSAT) oversees OTP accreditation standards and certification
- **Charitable Choice (42 CFR Part 54):** Applies to states, local governments, and religious organizations that receive SUBG or PATH homelessness formula grant funds
- **HHS Good Guidance Practices Regulation:** Effective January 6, 2021; any interested party may petition HHS to withdraw or modify a guidance document

## Protection and advocacy (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: PAIMI Program](https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/serious-mental-illness/paimi)
- **PAIMI Act:** The Protection and Advocacy for Individuals with Mental Illness (PAIMI) Act of 1986 authorizes grants to protect and advocate for the rights of adults with SMI and children with SED
- Purpose of PAIMI: provide legal-based advocacy services, protect and advocate for rights, and investigate abuse, neglect, and rights violations
- PAIMI grantees are 57 state Protection and Advocacy (P&A) systems: the 50 states, the District of Columbia, 5 territories, and the American Indian Consortium
- The Developmental Disabilities Assistance and Bill of Rights Act of 1975 (DD Act) requires reports at least every two years to the President, Congress, and the National Council on Disability

## Olmstead v. L.C. (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Olmstead v. L.C. Resources](https://www.samhsa.gov/about/laws-regulations/olmstead)
- **Olmstead v. L.C. (1999):** The Supreme Court held that unjustified institutional isolation of people with disabilities can constitute discrimination under Title II of the ADA when community placement is appropriate, not opposed by the individual, and can be reasonably accommodated
- Factors the Court discussed: professional assessments, the individual's wishes, available state resources, and the needs of others receiving services
- June 2026: the Department of Justice Office of Legal Counsel concluded that neither Title II of the ADA nor Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act requires states to provide treatment in the most integrated setting appropriate, and that Olmstead did not establish such a broad integration mandate

## Block grants (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Substance Use and Mental Health Block Grants](https://www.samhsa.gov/grants/block-grants)
- **Block grant:** A noncompetitive, formula grant mandated by the U.S. Congress; eligible entities submit an annual application showing statutory and regulatory compliance to receive the formula-based funding
- SAMHSA is responsible for two block grant programs: the MHBG and the SUBG
- Block grants supplement Medicaid, Medicare, and private insurance services
- Block grant purposes: fund priority treatment and support services for people without insurance or with short coverage gaps; fund successful services not covered by Medicaid, Medicare, or private insurance; fund primary prevention (universal, selective, and indicated); and collect performance and outcome data
- **Community Mental Health Services Block Grant (MHBG):** Funds comprehensive, community-based mental health services for adults with serious mental illnesses and children with serious emotional disturbances
  - MHBG funding must be used to serve children with SED and adults with SMI (SMI and SED were originally defined in a Federal Register notice in May 1993)
  - MHBG grantees must form and support a state or territory mental health planning council; not less than 50 percent of council members should be people who are not state employees or providers of mental health services
  - Each year, the Center for Mental Health Services (CMHS) conducts site visits of at least ten MHBG grantees
  - Many states are moving from mental health planning councils to behavioral health planning councils
- **Substance Use Prevention, Treatment, and Recovery Services Block Grant (SUPTRS BG or SUBG):** Helps plan, implement, and evaluate activities that prevent and treat substance use; authorized by section 1921 of Title XIX of the PHS Act
  - Administered by CSAT's State Systems Partnership Branch with CSAP's Division of Primary Prevention
  - SUBG targeted populations and service areas: pregnant women and women with dependent children, injection drug users, tuberculosis services, early intervention services for HIV/AIDS, and primary prevention services
  - Grantees must spend no less than 20% of their SUBG allotment on primary prevention strategies for people not identified as needing treatment
  - SUBG allotments are based on the Population-at-Risk, Cost-of-Services, and Fiscal Capacity Indexes (the MHBG uses a Weighted Population-at-Risk Index)

## Synar Amendment (SAMHSA)
- **Synar Amendment:** Part of the 1992 ADAMHA Reorganization Act (PL 102-321); requires states to enact and enforce laws prohibiting the sale or distribution of tobacco products to anyone under the age of 18, to decrease youth access to tobacco
- Synar requires states to: enact laws prohibiting tobacco sales and distribution to minors; enforce them to reduce youth access; conduct random, unannounced inspections of tobacco outlets; and report annual findings to the HHS Secretary by December 31 each year
- SAMHSA's CSAP oversees Synar through the Tobacco/Synar Program and can withhold SUBG funds from states that do not comply
- The Synar tobacco regulation final rule was published January 19, 1996

## Workplace rights (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Support for the Workplace](https://www.samhsa.gov/find-support/how-to-cope/workplace)
- **Parity:** Under the MHPAEA, the legal requirement that job-based health plans and health insurance issuers provide mental health benefits in a similar way as medical and surgical benefits
- If coverage for mental health, drug, or alcohol treatment is denied, you can ask the insurance company to re-review (appeal) its decision
- **ADA (workplace):** A national law saying you cannot treat people unfairly because of a disability; it covers many disabilities, including mental health, drug, and alcohol issues
- **Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA):** Protections when you need time off to manage your serious health condition, or that of a spouse, child, or parent, including mental health or substance use treatment
- **Employee assistance program (EAP):** Some employers offer an EAP or workplace wellness program with programs to lower stress, including free or low-cost counseling

## Buprenorphine rules: MAT Act and MATE Act (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Waiver Elimination (MAT Act)](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/treatment/resources/mat-act) · [Statutes, Regulations, and Guidelines](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/treatment/statutes-regulations-guidelines)
- **MAT Act (waiver elimination):** Section 1262 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023 (the Omnibus bill) removed the federal requirement to submit a Notice of Intent (have a waiver) to prescribe buprenorphine for opioid use disorder (OUD)
  - It also removed discipline restrictions, patient limits, and counseling certification tied to the waiver; there are no longer any patient caps
  - Any practitioner with a current DEA registration that includes Schedule III authority may prescribe buprenorphine for OUD if state law permits
  - The DATA 2000 waiver requirement was removed in December 2022; as of June 1, 2026, SAMHSA decommissioned the Behavioral Waiver Notification System (BWNS)
- **MATE Act (training requirement):** Section 1263 requires new or renewing DEA registrants, starting June 27, 2023, to have one of: a total of eight hours of training on opioid or other substance use disorders; board certification in addiction medicine or addiction psychiatry; or graduation within five years from a U.S. medical, advanced practice nursing, or physician assistant school with an SUD curriculum of at least eight hours
- Buprenorphine is a Schedule III medication and can be prescribed by telehealth if all federal, state, and local laws are followed
- The 2023 changes do not affect OUD treatment with methadone
- Counseling is strongly recommended but not required to prescribe buprenorphine; medication should not be made contingent on participation in counseling
- Standard OUD screening involves clinical interviewing, urine or oral fluid toxicology testing, and the Clinical Opioid Withdrawal Scale
- Person-centered care means assessing a person's stage of change, including the patient's goals, and using shared decision-making
