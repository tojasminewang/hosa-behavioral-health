## Sensation and perception
- **Sensation:** detecting stimuli from the environment or the body; shaped by natural selection for survival; differences between people are subtle (e.g., needing glasses)
- **Perception:** interpreting sensory information; varies more between people, since the same stimulus can be interpreted differently
- **Stimulus:** anything that triggers a sensory reaction; external (scent of a flower) or internal (pain of a sprained ankle)
- **Transduction:** turning sensory information into neural signals sent to the brain
- Perception lets us organize, recognize, and use sensory information
- Attention is a gateway to perception; unfamiliar, changing, or intense stimuli get priority because they can matter for safety
- **Sensory adaptation:** paying less attention to an unchanging stimulus
- **Divided attention:** processing several sources at once
- **Selective attention:** focusing on a few stimuli and ignoring the rest; can cause errors
- **Bottom-up processing:** perception built from new incoming signals
- **Top-down processing:** perception shaped by knowledge, memories, expectations
- Familiar situations use more top-down processing, always alongside bottom-up

## Thresholds
- **Psychophysics:** Gustav Fechner's method relating stimuli's physical qualities to subjective responses
- **Absolute threshold:** weakest stimulus detected at least 50% of the time
- **Difference threshold:** smallest difference between two stimuli detected at least 50% of the time
- **Signal detection:** judging faint, uncertain stimuli; depends on the stimulus's intensity and the observer's criteria (maximize hits or minimize misses)

## Vision
- **Vision:** processes the visible part of the light spectrum (not gamma, x-ray, ultraviolet, infrared, microwave, radio)
- ~50% of the cortex processes vision (vs ~3% hearing, ~11% touch and pain)
- **Cornea:** clear front surface; starts bending light toward the retina
- **Iris:** coloured muscle ring; adjusts the pupil to light and to autonomic signals
- **Pupil:** the opening formed by the iris
- **Lens:** clear structure behind the pupil; bends light to the retina
- **Accommodation:** changing the lens's shape to focus near or far
- **Retina:** photoreceptor layers at the back of the eye that transduce light

| Structure | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Rods | Dim light; no colour; blurry image; ~90 million per eye; mostly in the periphery |
| Cones | Colour and fine detail; best in bright light; 4–5 million per eye; concentrated in the fovea |
| Fovea | Detailed central vision; mostly cones |
| Optic disk | Where the optic nerve exits; no rods or cones, so a blind spot the brain fills in |

![Structures of the eye](hc-eye)

- Optic nerves partly cross at the optic chiasm (axons nearest the nose cross); right hemisphere sees the left visual field, left hemisphere the right
- Past the chiasm = optic tracts; ~90% of axons → thalamus → amygdala (quick judgements) and primary visual cortex (occipital lobe); ~10% → hypothalamus (sleep–wake) or superior colliculi (visual reflexes)
- **Trichromatic theory:** three cone types for short (blue), medium (green), long (red) wavelengths; explains colour deficiencies but not afterimages; best fits the cones
- **Colour afterimage:** seeing the complementary colour after staring at one colour
- **Opponent process theory:** red-green and blue-yellow channels, each colour inhibiting its partner; explains afterimages; best fits perception

## Gestalt and depth
- **Gestalt principles:** German psychologists' rules for grouping; the whole is more than the sum of its parts
- **Organization principle:** split a scene into figure and background
- **Proximity principle:** close objects are grouped
- **Continuity principle:** points forming smooth lines are grouped
- **Closure principle:** we "fill in the blanks" in gapped images
- **Simplicity principle (Prägnanz):** we choose the simplest solution

![Five Gestalt principles. Organization: is it a vase, or two faces?](hc-gestalt)

- **Depth perception:** seeing 3D from the 2D retinal image
- **Monocular cues:** one eye: occlusion (near blocks far), relative size (closer = bigger), motion parallax (closer moves past faster)

![Two monocular depth cues in one picture](hc-depth-cues)

- **Binocular cues:** need both eyes
- **Retinal disparity:** difference between the two eyes' images; grows with distance between objects

## Hearing
- **Audition:** detects sound waves; identifying objects, language
- **Amplitude:** sound intensity in decibels (dB); higher = louder
- **Frequency:** pitch in hertz (Hz); higher = higher pitch
- **Pinna:** collects and focuses sound; locates sounds above or below
- **Tympanic membrane (eardrum):** boundary of outer and middle ear; vibrates the middle-ear ossicles, which pass sound to the oval window
- **Oval window:** passes vibrations into the inner ear's fluid
- **Cochlea:** inner-ear structure with the auditory receptors; three chambers (vestibular canal, tympanic canal, cochlear duct); waves travel around its apex and back
- **Basilar membrane:** separates the cochlear duct; holds the organ of Corti, whose hair cells transduce sound

![The outer, middle and inner ear](hc-ear)

- Route: auditory nerve → medulla → midbrain (sound reflexes) → thalamus → primary auditory cortex → secondary auditory cortex (complex sounds)
- High frequencies move the basilar membrane most near its base; low frequencies near its apex
- Frequency affects perceived loudness; hearing is most sensitive at 80–10 000 Hz
- Locating sounds: vertically by the pinna; horizontally by comparing loudness and timing at each ear

## Body senses
- **Somatosensory stimuli:** body position and movement, touch, temperature, pain; feedback for movement, speech, safety
- **Vestibular system:** three inner-ear channels for head position and movement; hair cells bend in fluid, signalling via the auditory nerve
- Vestibular route: medulla and cerebellum; medulla → thalamus → somatosensory cortex (parietal) → motor cortex (frontal)
- Touch confirms vision and is social communication in infants; skin neurons respond to pressure or vibration; receptors also in blood vessels, joints, organs
- Touch route: receptors → spinal cord → thalamus → primary somatosensory cortex (parietal lobe)
- Greatest touch sensitivity: hands and mouth; the brain has no touch or pain receptors
- **Free nerve endings:** pain receptors triggered by tissue damage (mechanical, temperature, chemical)
- Pain: spinal cord → thalamus (fast, direct) → anterior cingulate cortex (pain's emotional side)
- **Gate theory of pain:** actions and higher thinking affect pain by competing with pain signals
- **Placebo effect:** less pain after an ineffective substance or treatment

## Smell and taste
- **Olfaction:** smell; nose receptors detect airborne molecules; works at a distance
- **Gustation:** taste; tongue receptors detect molecules dissolved in saliva; needs contact
- Both warn of danger (smoke) and add to emotional and social life (perfume)
- Principles: many complex mixtures identified; small differences detected; experience shapes perception
- Odourants meet olfactory receptors in the nasal cavities; receptors die and are replaced; mucus dissolves odourants
- **Olfactory nerve:** carries smell signals to the olfactory bulbs
- **Olfactory bulb:** one of two structures below the frontal lobes receiving input from the nose
- Smell skips direct contact with the thalamus: bulb → olfactory cortex → amygdala (emotion)
- Taste protects us from spoiled or poisonous food
- Taste receptors are all over the tongue and mouth, each sensitive to all tastes; the "tongue map" is a myth
- **Papillae:** tongue bumps holding taste buds
- **Taste bud:** holds taste receptors, which are regularly replaced

![Where smell and taste begin](hc-smell-taste)

- Taste route: mouth → medulla → thalamus → somatosensory cortex → orbitofrontal cortex (emotion of taste)

## Development and experience
- Infants see everything adults do but with less detail, so they need more contrast
- With age, the lens accommodates more slowly (slower brightness adjustment), iris muscles stiffen, pupils stay smaller, the lens yellows (changes colour perception)
- Fetuses hear; newborns recognize their mother's voice
- Age-related hearing loss starts at high frequencies
- **Perfect pitch:** naming a heard musical tone; larger left-hemisphere areas in musicians who have it; can be trained
- Pain is shaped more than any other sense by culture, context, experience
- Prepared-childbirth classes → less reported pain; contact-sport athletes tolerate more pain; self-administered morphine → less medication needed
- Taste buds and smell decline with age; females are more sensitive to smell
- Chemical-sense problems are linked to psychological disorders (e.g., smells can trigger bad memories in PTSD)
