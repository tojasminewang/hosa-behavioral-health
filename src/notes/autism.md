## Autism spectrum disorder (textbook) {pp. 555–558}
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD):** A disorder characterized by deficits in social relatedness and communication skills that are often accompanied by repetitive, ritualistic behavior
- The word autism literally means "within oneself"; psychiatrist Leo Kanner described the condition in 1943
- ASD is a neurodevelopmental disorder; the DSM-5 category combines the previous categories of autism and Asperger's syndrome
- It is a "spectrum" because severity varies widely, from relatively normal adjustment and independent living to intellectual disability requiring care
- About 1 child out of every 54 has ASD; boys are diagnosed 4.3 times more often than girls; rates have increased rapidly over two decades (possibly due to awareness, relaxed criteria, and services)
- Educational verification by school teams produces higher rates than medical/psychological diagnosis
- Problems with social relatedness are at the core: most children with ASD do not make eye contact or enjoy reciprocal games like peekaboo
- A failure to develop a normal **theory of mind** (understanding others' thoughts, normally by ages 3–4) might explain many social deficits
- Language ranges from none, to delayed, to normal; even with normal language, maintaining conversations is difficult
- Individuals object strongly to changes in their environment and show repetitive, routine behavior (rocking, hand flapping, head banging, twirling) or extremely limited preoccupations
- Most show unusually increased or decreased sensitivity to stimuli (may be insensitive to pain or cold but distressed by normal sounds); auditory processing and absolute pitch are more common
- Many individuals with ASD are oversensitive to touch and may reject hugs and cuddling

## Causes of ASD (textbook) {pp. 556–558}
- Strong genetic influence: concordance between identical twins may be 76% to 88% or more; hundreds of genes are probably involved
- In typical brains 174 genes are expressed differently in frontal vs. temporal lobes, but in ASD brains none were
- **Minicolumns:** Vertical arrays of neurons, the smallest processing units of the cortex; people with ASD have narrower minicolumns spaced farther apart, favoring detailed focus over global processing
- Structural differences have been seen in the amygdala, hippocampus, and cerebellum; the "broken mirror system" idea has little support
- Environmental risks: older parental age; prenatal infection, pesticides, nutritional factors, and drugs; maternal cannabis use; markers of prenatal acetaminophen exposure; prenatal antidepressant exposure is debated
- Vaccines do not cause ASD: a meta-analysis of over 1 million children found no link with vaccination, the MMR vaccine, or thimerosal; the original Lancet paper was retracted
- Parents withholding vaccines led to more cases of preventable diseases (measles cases doubled in early 2008, all in unvaccinated children)
- The only effective treatment for ASD today is behavioral therapy (e.g., O. Ivar Lovaas's applied behavior analysis); it does not cure ASD but typically improves functioning
- Infants at high risk who were superior at visual search at 9 months showed more ASD symptoms later; abnormal amygdala development is one of the most consistent biological correlates

## Treating ASD (textbook) {pp. 618–620}
- No medications have proved effective for the core symptoms of ASD; two antipsychotics are FDA-approved for severe "irritability" (aggression, tantrums, self-injury) but cause weight gain, diabetes, and motor problems
- Antidepressants and other drugs are often prescribed **off-label** (not officially approved for this use)
- The most commonly used and most effective psychological treatment is **applied behavior analysis (ABA)**, built on operant conditioning; early intervention improves IQ, language, and daily living
- Unsupported "cures" include gluten- and casein-free diets, chelation (one child died), and hyperbaric oxygen; they are expensive and distract from ABA
- **Neurodiversity:** The view that ASD is a difference rather than a disorder to be fixed; it advocates building strengths ("We are fresh water fish in salt water")

## Autism (NAMI)
@ [NAMI: Autism](https://www.nami.org/co-occurring-conditions/autism/)
- NAMI describes autism spectrum disorder (ASD) as a developmental condition that affects socializing and communicating, often with restricted or repetitive behaviors, interests or activities
- "Spectrum" refers to how much symptoms, behaviors and severity vary; some people are mildly impaired and others are severely disabled
- NAMI cites CDC surveillance that an average of 1 in every 59 8-year-old U.S. children has ASD, and that boys are four times more likely than girls to develop symptoms
- ASD symptoms usually appear in the first three years of life; parents are often the first to notice unusual behaviors such as poor eye contact or not responding to their name
- NAMI ASD signs: delayed language, repetitive routines, difficulty with eye contact, sensory dysregulation, trouble reading facial expressions, problems expressing emotions, fixation on parts of objects, absence of pretend play, self-injury and sleep problems
- **Sensory dysregulation:** In ASD, being over-sensitive (hyper) or less sensitive (hypo) to sights, sounds, smells, textures or tastes, which can cause sensory overload and meltdowns
- Causes of ASD: genetics (siblings and identical twins are more likely to share it; brain structure and chemistry differences) and environmental factors; strong evidence shows vaccines do not cause autism
- ASD diagnosis is often a 2-stage process: developmental screening at well-child checkups, then a thorough evaluation by a team of specialists; children can be reliably diagnosed by age two
- ASD screening tools for toddlers and preschoolers:
  - **M-CHAT (Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers):** Questions whose answers show whether a child should be evaluated further by a specialist
  - **STAT (Screening Tool for Autism in Two-Year-Olds):** Supervised tasks that assess social and communication behaviors such as imitation, play and directing attention
  - **SCQ (Social Communication Questionnaire):** Questions parents answer to decide if a child aged 4 or older needs more testing
  - **CSBS (Communication and Symbolic Behavior Scales):** Parent interviews plus observation of natural play to assess communication development
- There is no known cure for ASD, but effective treatment includes education and skills training (with speech-language and occupational therapists), behavioral treatments such as ABA, medication for co-occurring symptoms, and complementary approaches
- **Applied behavior analysis (ABA):** One of the most researched autism therapies; it rewards positive behavior (with praise or a small toy) and uses prompts when the child does not respond
- **Floor time:** A therapy that builds speech, motor and cognitive skills through emotional development in interactive play between parents and children
- No FDA-approved medications treat the core symptoms of ASD; two antipsychotics, aripiprazole and risperidone, are approved for autism-related irritability
- Conditions that can occur with autism: intellectual impairment, seizures (1 in 4 children with autism; confirmed with an EEG), Fragile X syndrome, tuberous sclerosis, gastrointestinal problems, and mental health conditions such as anxiety, ADHD and depression
- **Fragile X syndrome:** The most common inherited form of intellectual impairment; about 1 in 3 children with Fragile X meet ASD criteria, and about 1 in 25 children with ASD have the Fragile X mutation
- **Rett syndrome:** A developmental disorder that mostly affects girls, with normal development for 6–18 months followed by regression and ASD-like symptoms
- Complementary approaches for ASD: melatonin to help regulate sleep, multivitamins, and gluten- and casein-free diets that some families report help behavior
- NAMI notes that for adults with ASD, the negative associations and stigma from others can be more damaging than the symptoms themselves

## HOSA Canada study deck: treating autism
@ HOSA Canada Behavioural Health study deck "Prevention, Identification, Recovery & Management of Substance Abuse & Mental Illness" (HOSA Canada app, Module 4)
- ASD involves abnormalities in serotonin, GABA and glutamate, but no medications have been proved effective for its core symptoms
- ABA, the most common psychological treatment for ASD, is based on operant conditioning and targets observable, socially important behaviors such as language, aiming for improvements that carry over outside therapy
