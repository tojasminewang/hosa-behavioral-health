## Defining and diagnosing disorders
- Causes draw on genetic, biological, cognitive, developmental, and social threads
- **Psychological disorder:** syndrome of clinically significant disturbance in thinking, emotion regulation, or behaviour, reflecting dysfunction in psychological, biological, or developmental processes
- **Abnormal psychology:** the study of psychological disorders
- **Abnormal behaviour:** behaviour that is not typical, usual, or regular
- Telling typical from abnormal is the first step toward labelling and treatment
- Intellectual disability uses statistics: IQ of 70 or below is the cutoff
- A purely statistical approach implies an unwanted cultural specificity and misses a disorder's distress
- **Comorbid disorders:** more than one disorder at once, so adding up each disorder's rate doesn't work
- Diagnosis rests only on observable behaviour
- **Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM):** manual for consistent diagnosis
- Combining perspectives shows how causes interact; richer understanding → better treatments

## Childhood-onset disorders
- **Neurodevelopmental disorders:** diagnosed in childhood, often lifelong; ASD and ADHD are two of the most common
- **Autism spectrum disorder (ASD):** deficits in social relatedness and communication, often with repetitive, ritualistic behaviour
- ASD: genetic influence plus interacting environmental factors; vaccines play no part
- **Attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD):** unusual inattention, hyperactivity-impulsivity, or both
- ADHD: possibly genetic, with environmental factors such as low birth weight and exposure to tobacco, alcohol, other drugs; frontal lobes may be underactive

## Schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, depression
- **Schizophrenia:** hallucinations, delusions, disorganized thinking and speech, movement problems, restricted affect, avolition (no motivation) or asociality (no social interest)
- **Delusions:** false, illogical beliefs
- **Hallucinations:** false perceptions
- Schizophrenia: ~1% of people worldwide; grossly disorganized or abnormal movement
- Strong genetic vulnerability; many genes, overlapping with bipolar disorder
- Extreme stress affects onset and severity in vulnerable people; 5 times more common in lower than higher socioeconomic groups (poverty stress may trigger it); prenatal environment matters
- **Bipolar disorder:** mood disorder alternating mania and depression; 2.6% of American adults yearly
- **Mania:** unrealistically elevated mood
- Bipolar: strong genetic role; diet may interact (omega-3 fatty acids, usually in fish, protect); may be overrepresented among artistic, creative people
- **Major depressive disorder:** long low mood, lost pleasure, sleep and appetite changes, poor concentration, hopelessness, possible suicidal thoughts
- MDD: 7% of adults yearly; ages 18–29 have three times the rate of people over 60; more common in women
- Theories range from learning, social, and cognitive to biological
- Learning view: less positive reinforcement or more negative outcomes
- Cognitive view: negative thoughts about the self, the world, the future
- **Learned helplessness:** helplessness, possibly depression, after random or uncontrollable consequences
- Genes involved include serotonin genes; severe stress often comes right before an episode; biology interacts with thinking patterns and life events

## Anxiety and related disorders
- **Anxiety disorders:** anxiety out of proportion to circumstances
- Anxiety = strong negative emotions + physical tension from expecting danger
- ~30% of Americans experience one; anxiety does not stop realistic thinking
- **Specific phobia:** fear of objects other than those in agoraphobia or social anxiety disorder
- **Social anxiety disorder:** unrealistic fear of being scrutinized and criticized; more common in collectivistic cultures
- **Panic attack:** intense fear with autonomic arousal
- **Panic disorder:** repeated panic attacks plus fear of more
- **Agoraphobia:** fear of open spaces, from Greek agora, "marketplace"
- **Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD):** excessive anxiety and worry for 6 months, not tied to specific objects or situations
- US: lower socioeconomic classes are twice as likely to be diagnosed as upper and middle classes
- Overall, anxiety is likeliest with a high-reactive temperament from one's genes
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder (OCD):** intrusive obsessions and compulsions
- **Obsession:** intrusive, upsetting thought, unlike everyday worries about real-life problems
- **Compulsion:** repetitive, ritualistic behaviour tied to high anxiety
- OCD: strong genetic vulnerability; may follow head injury; learning views focus on rewarding compulsions; culture shapes frequency and content
- **Body dysmorphic disorder:** unrealistic perception of physical flaws
- **Posttraumatic stress disorder (PTSD):** the only disorder with a specific cause: an experienced trauma
- PTSD symptoms: hypervigilance, avoiding reminders, emotional numbing, reliving via intrusive thoughts, flashbacks, dreams
- PTSD: ~3%–4% of US adults; lower natural benzodiazepine activity in the frontal cortex; conditioned responses to trauma cues; social and cultural factors matter
- **Dissociative disorders:** disrupted identity, memory, or consciousness
- **Dissociative amnesia:** forgetting important information about specific events
- **Depersonalization/derealization disorder:** strong feeling that the self or surroundings are unreal
- **Somatic symptom disorder:** physical symptoms with no medical cause that seem serious and disabling to the patient, with excessive worry and often high anxiety
- Somatic symptoms: vague pain, gut upset, sexual problems, amnesia, breathing problems, unexplained sensory or movement problems

## Personality disorders
- **Personality disorders:** impaired identity, personality traits, empathy or intimacy
- **Antisocial personality disorder (ASPD):** pervasive disregard for and violation of others' rights; close to the term "psychopath"; mainly genetic per research; orbitofrontal cortex abnormalities
- **Borderline personality disorder (BPD):** unstable relationships, self-image, emotion; trauma interacting with genetic tendency
- **Narcissistic personality disorder (NPD):** grandiosity, need for admiration, lack of empathy
- **Grandiose narcissist:** inflated self-worth with aggressiveness and dominance
- **Vulnerable narcissist:** inflated self-worth with insecurity, defensiveness, negative emotions

## Providing therapy
- Clinical psychology integrates perspectives; treatments are psychological or biological
- **Psychotherapy:** treating symptoms through therapist–client conversation
- **Counseling:** humanistic, strength-focused origin; now treatment for adjustment problems rather than severe disorders
- **Evidence-based practice (EBP):** clinician's experience + best scientific evidence + patient's values and expectations

| Professional | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Psychotherapist | Licensed professional who provides psychotherapy |
| Psychiatrist | Medical doctor in psychiatry; can prescribe medication |
| Psychologist | Advanced degree in psychology (PhD or PsyD); clinical psychologists also complete a supervised treatment internship |
| Counselor | PhD, PsyD, EdD, or master's degree; specializes in adjustment problems |

| Format | What it is |
|---|---|
| Brief therapy | Short-term psychotherapy, about 3 to 5 sessions |
| Group therapy | A group of 6 to 12 people |
| Family therapy | Family members take part alone and together |
| Couples therapy | Family therapy focused on intimate relationships |

- Challenge: large organizations such as insurers, not small private practices, increasingly manage treatment: which disorders are treated and its length, frequency, type

## Psychological therapies
- **Insight therapy:** improving symptoms by building understanding of one's situation
- **Psychoanalysis:** Freud's insight therapy, used by followers of psychodynamic theories
- **Free association:** saying whatever comes to mind without censoring
- **Transference:** using the patient's reactions to the therapist to understand their approach to authority figures
- **Resistance:** reluctance to accept interpretations, taken as a sign they are correct
- **Person-centered therapy:** Carl Rogers's humanistic therapy aiming for congruence of the real and ideal selves
- **Behaviour therapy:** classical and operant conditioning applied to symptoms and adjustment problems
- **Cognitive therapy:** changing how people think about situations so it no longer causes abnormal behaviour
- **Cognitive restructuring:** replacing irrational beliefs with rational ones
- **Cognitive behavioural therapy (CBT):** cognitive restructuring + behavioural treatment
- **Biopsychosocial approach:** treating biological, personal, and social roots together

## Biological therapies
- **Medication:** preferred more by younger patients and women
- **Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT):** for severe depression unresponsive to other treatment; anesthesia and a muscle relaxant, then electrodes on the head induce a generalized seizure
- **Psychosurgery:** brain surgery to improve symptoms
- **Deep brain stimulation:** surgically implanted electrodes; anxiety and mood disorders
- **Neurofeedback:** biofeedback teaching patients to keep brain-activity measures in a range; ADHD and seizures

| Disorder | Psychological treatment | Biological treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Autism spectrum disorder (ASD) | Applied behaviour analysis | Antipsychotics for self-injury |
| ADHD | Behaviour therapy | Stimulants |
| Schizophrenia | Psychosocial rehabilitation; evaluating expressed emotion | Antipsychotic drugs |
| Bipolar disorder | Supportive CBT | Lithium carbonate; ECT for depression |
| Major depressive disorder | CBT | Antidepressants, ECT, exercise |
| Anxiety disorders, OCD, body dysmorphic disorder, PTSD | CBT | Antidepressant or anti-anxiety medication |
| Personality disorders | Learning models (ASPD), DBT (borderline), CBT (NPD) | No effective biological treatments |
