## Kinds of behaviour and learning
- **Reflexes:** automatic, involuntary, fast, reliable responses that protect us (goosebumps from stress or cold, left over from hairy ancestors)
- **Instincts (fixed action patterns):** inborn patterns set off by the environment; run to completion once started; need more neurons than reflexes (e.g., contagious yawning, possibly linked to empathy)
- **Learned behaviours:** relatively permanent changes in behaviour (or capacity for it) from experience
- **Associative learning:** linking stimuli and behaviours; helps predict the future from the past
- **Classical conditioning:** links two stimuli that occur one after the other
- **Operant conditioning:** links behaviours and their outcomes
- **Nonassociative learning:** changes in how strongly we respond to a stimulus
- **Habituation:** weaker responses to a repeated, unchanging, harmless stimulus
- **Sensitization:** stronger reactions to many stimuli after one strong stimulus (jumpy after an earthquake)
- **Observational learning:** learning by watching another organism; also called social learning or modeling

## Classical conditioning
- "Conditioned" = learned; "unconditioned" = no learning needed
- **Unconditioned stimulus (UCS):** triggers a response without experience
- **Unconditioned response (UCR):** unlearned response to the UCS
- **Conditioned stimulus (CS):** event whose meaning is learned
- **Conditioned response (CR):** learned response
- Pavlov: before, food (UCS) → salivation (UCR) and a metronome → nothing; during, metronome then food; after, metronome alone (CS) → salivation (CR)
- **Acquisition:** developing a CR; needs contiguity (CS and UCS close in time) and contingency (CS and UCS correlated)
- **Extinction:** a CR weakening once the UCS stops following the CS; new learning, not forgetting
- **Spontaneous recovery:** a CR returning after rest during extinction training
- **Inhibition:** a CS predicting that the UCS won't happen; inhibitory CSs tied to an absent drug effect might aid addiction recovery
- **Generalization:** responding to stimuli similar to the CS
- **Discrimination:** learned ability to tell stimuli apart
- **Higher-order conditioning:** stimuli paired with a CS also trigger the CR
- **Latent inhibition:** slower learning when the CS is already familiar
- **Rescorla-Wagner model (1972):** learning depends on how surprising the CS–UCS link is
- **Taste aversion:** Garcia and Robert Koelling (1966): the kinds of stimuli matter; some pairings are learned faster
- These findings moved psychology from rigid behaviourism toward cognitive, biological, and evolutionary approaches

## Uses of classical conditioning
- **Aversion therapy:** counterconditioning that re-pairs a CS from a pleasant UCS with an unpleasant UCS
- **Systematic desensitization:** counterconditioning: relaxing while exposed to feared things
- **Exposure therapy:** safe exposure to feared things until the reaction stops (phobias)
- Addiction: drug cues (CSs) linked to drug effects (UCSs) keep triggering craving (CR)
- Conditioning helps form and change attitudes and prejudice
- Latent inhibition focuses us on novelty by reducing learning about the familiar; linked to creativity and schizophrenia

## Operant conditioning
- **Law of effect:** Edward Thorndike (cats in "puzzle boxes"): behaviour is "stamped in" depending on its consequences
- B. F. Skinner extended this with the Skinner box

| Consequence | What happens | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Positive reinforcement | A desired outcome is given | Increases |
| Negative reinforcement | Behaviour escapes or avoids something unpleasant | Increases |
| Positive punishment | An aversive stimulus is applied | Decreases or stops |
| Negative punishment | Something desirable is removed | Decreases |

- **Premack principle:** what an organism spends the most time and energy on is likely important to it
- **Conditioned reinforcer (secondary reinforcer):** gains value from links to valued things (money, grades)
- **Partial reinforcement:** reinforcing only some occurrences

| Schedule | Reinforcement comes |
|---|---|
| Fixed ratio (FR) | After a set number of behaviours |
| Variable ratio (VR) | After a changing number of behaviours |
| Fixed interval (FI) | For the first response after a set time |
| Variable interval (VI) | For the first response after a changing time |

- **Partial reinforcement effect in extinction:** behaviour learned with continuous reinforcement dies out faster than with partial reinforcement
- **Shaping (method of successive approximations):** reinforce behaviours close to a rare target, then gradually raise the standard
- **Chaining:** breaking a complex behaviour into manageable steps
- **Latent learning:** learning without reinforcement
- **Instinctive drift:** instinctive behaviours interfering with training
- Social interaction gives the arousal, focus, and motivation that help learning
- **Token economy:** tokens traded for reinforcers to increase desirable behaviour (like money)
- Behaviour therapies also apply operant conditioning

## Observational learning
- **Imitation:** copying behaviour unlikely to occur on its own
- **Bobo doll studies:** Bandura: children who watched an adult attack a Bobo doll copied the adult's movements and words
- Modeling needs attention, retention, reproduction, motivation
- Imitation in monkeys and humans involves mirror neurons, possibly the basis of empathy
- Culture transmits learning
