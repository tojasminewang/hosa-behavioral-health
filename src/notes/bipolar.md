## Bipolar disorder (textbook) {p. 567}
- **Bipolar disorder:** A mood disorder characterized by alternating periods of mania and depression; DSM-5 calls it "a bridge between psychotic disorders, such as schizophrenia, and depressive disorders"
- Bipolar disorder features a period of mania, which may or may not be followed by a period of depression; its depressive symptoms are identical to those of major depressive disorder
- **Mania:** A period of unrealistically elevated mood; "a distinct period of abnormally and persistently elevated, expansive, or irritable mood, abnormally and persistently increased goal-directed activity or energy"
- Manic symptoms: grandiosity (feeling unrealistically special or important), little need for sleep, rapid speech, difficulty concentrating, and rapidly shifting ideas
- Unlike many disorders, mania increases productive, goal-directed behavior, but "excessive involvement in activities that have a high potential for painful consequences" often requires hospitalization for protection
- Bipolar disorder affects about 2.6% of American adults each year; women are diagnosed more than men by about 3:2; average age of onset is 25 years
- Children and youth under 18 with bipolar-like symptoms are diagnosed with **disruptive mood dysregulation disorder**
- Genetics: identical twin concordance is reported as high as 70%; there is considerable genetic overlap with schizophrenia (one set of identical triplets had two with schizophrenia and one with bipolar disorder)
- Bipolar disorder is linked to oxidative stress and accelerated aging, with brain changes similar to dementia; current treatments have antioxidant effects
- Omega-3 fatty acids (in fish) may protect: rates are highest where fish is rarely eaten (Germany) and lowest where it is a staple (Iceland); patients have low omega-3 levels
- Bipolar disorder may be overrepresented among creative people (possibly William Blake, Handel, Mahler, Van Gogh, Michelangelo; diagnosed: Carrie Fisher, Vivien Leigh); patients score like healthy creative professionals on creativity tests
- People with bipolar disorder have an abnormally low need for sleep, including REM sleep; abnormal norepinephrine activity is implicated
- People with bipolar disorder recalled words best when tested in the same mood state (mania or depression) in which they learned them

## Treating bipolar disorder (textbook) {pp. 600, 624}
- In 1949 Australian psychiatrist John Cade discovered that lithium salts improved bipolar disorder (approved in the U.S. in 1970; lithium is an element, so it could not be patented)
- **Lithium carbonate** is the most commonly prescribed medication; it prevents recurrences of mania and is uniquely preventive of suicidal behavior
- Lithium protects against neurodegeneration, repairs oxidative stress, resets circadian rhythms, and increases gray matter
- Toxic lithium levels cause nausea, vomiting, tremor, coma, and seizures, so regular blood tests are needed; patients with bipolar disorder are less likely to follow treatment instructions than any other patients
- Antipsychotics are also used but seem less effective than lithium; omega-3 fatty acids may help without side effects
- Psychotherapy alone is not typically an option, but therapy helping patients and families cope and adhere to treatment is beneficial

## Bipolar disorder (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Bipolar Disorder](https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/what-is-mental-health/conditions/bipolar)
- Bipolar disorder is a serious mental illness that causes unusual shifts in mood, from extreme highs (mania or "manic" episodes) to lows (depression or "depressive" episodes), with changes in energy, thinking, behavior, and sleep
- Most people are diagnosed in their teens or twenties, but it can occur at any age
- Higher risk: family history of bipolar disorder, a traumatic event, and/or misuse of drugs or alcohol; differences in brain structure and function may also play a role
- A severe manic or depressive episode may trigger psychotic symptoms such as delusions or hallucinations
- Manic episode signs: intense euphoria, appearing jumpy or wired, excessive energy, decreased need for sleep, fast or excessive talking, racing thoughts, distractibility, inflated self-esteem, impulsive or risky behavior (unsafe sex, spending a lot of money), agitation and irritability, hypomania
- Depressive episode signs: feeling sad, worthless, guilty, empty, or hopeless; lack of interest; low energy; forgetfulness; indecisiveness; trouble concentrating; sleep and appetite changes; thoughts of death or suicide

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Bipolar I disorder | A history of at least one manic episode, sometimes with depressed or hypomanic episodes as well |
| Bipolar II disorder | Highs are less extreme (hypomanic states); depressive episodes may be as severe as in MDD or bipolar I |
| Cyclothymic disorder | More chronic mood instability (highs and lows) that is not as long, severe, or frequent as in bipolar I or II |

![Mood patterns in three bipolar disorders (a sketch, not to scale)](bipolar-types)

- **Hypomanic state:** A high that is less extreme than full mania
- Bipolar disorder is a lifelong condition that doesn't go away on its own; it is treatable with a combination of medication (mood stabilizers, antipsychotics, antidepressants) and talk therapy, plus long-term continuous treatment
- Healthy habits: keep a consistent sleep schedule (sleep deprivation can trigger episodes), limit caffeine, eat well and exercise, take medicine even when mood is stable, keep a mood journal, avoid alcohol and drugs, minimize stress, and maintain a support network

## Bipolar disorder (NAMI)
@ [NAMI: Bipolar Disorder](https://www.nami.org/types-of-conditions/bipolar-disorder/)
- Bipolar disorder causes dramatic shifts in mood, energy and the ability to think clearly, with highs (mania) and lows (depression) that differ from typical ups and downs
- The average age of onset is about 25; bipolar disorder affects men and women equally, about 2.8% of the U.S. population is diagnosed, and nearly 83% of cases are classified as severe
- Untreated bipolar disorder usually gets worse; psychotherapy, medication, a healthy lifestyle, a regular schedule and early recognition of symptoms help many people live well
- A person may go years without symptoms, or may experience both extremes at the same time or in rapid sequence
- Severe episodes can include hallucinations or delusions that usually mirror the extreme mood, so some people with bipolar disorder are wrongly diagnosed with schizophrenia
- A bipolar diagnosis requires at least one episode of mania or hypomania
- **Hypomania:** A milder form of mania without psychotic episodes; people can often still function socially or at work
- During mania people often act impulsively, make reckless decisions and take unusual risks, usually without realizing the consequences; suicide is a danger even in manic states
- Bipolar depressive symptoms must be present nearly every day for at least two weeks for a diagnosis
- Causes: genetics (risk rises if a parent or sibling has it, though one identical twin may develop it while the other does not), stress (a death, divorce or financial problems can trigger an episode), and subtle differences in brain structure and function
- Brain scans and blood tests cannot diagnose bipolar disorder, but tests can rule out look-alike illnesses such as hyperthyroidism
- **Bipolar I disorder:** One or more manic episodes lasting at least 7 days or severe enough to require hospitalization (most people also have depressive episodes, but they are not required)
- **Bipolar II disorder:** Depressive episodes that shift back and forth with hypomanic episodes, but never a full manic episode
- **Cyclothymic disorder (cyclothymia):** Hypomania and mild depression for at least two years, with normal-mood periods that last less than eight weeks
- **Other specified and unspecified bipolar disorder:** Clinically significant mood elevation that does not meet the criteria for bipolar I, bipolar II or cyclothymia
- NAMI bipolar treatments: psychotherapy (CBT and family-focused therapy), medications (mood stabilizers, antipsychotics and, to a lesser extent, antidepressants), self-management, and complementary approaches that support but do not replace treatment
- **STEP-BD:** The largest study of bipolar treatments (Systematic Treatment Enhancement Program for Bipolar Disorder); it followed over 4,000 people and found that patients on medication got well faster and stayed well with intensive psychotherapy
- **Lithium:** A mood stabilizer that prevents extreme highs and lows; it needs periodic blood tests because it can cause thyroid and kidney problems and toxicity
  - Evidence suggests lithium can lower suicide risk, though the FDA has not approved it for that purpose
- Anticonvulsants (seizure medications) also work as mood stabilizers: valproic acid and carbamazepine treat mania about as well as lithium, and lamotrigine delays bipolar I episodes
- Second-generation antipsychotics treat manic or mixed episodes; only quetiapine and the olanzapine–fluoxetine combination (Symbyax) are approved for bipolar depression
- Antidepressants can trigger mania in some people; an NIMH study found that adding an antidepressant to a mood stabilizer worked no better than a mood stabilizer alone for bipolar I
- ECT may be considered in rare cases of severe mania or depression that have not responded to other treatments
- Antidepressants used for OCD and stimulants used for ADHD can worsen bipolar symptoms and even trigger mania; bipolar disorder can also be misdiagnosed as borderline personality disorder
- Children with bipolar disorder usually receive psychotherapy and psychosocial interventions before medication is considered
- **Disruptive mood dysregulation disorder (DMDD):** A diagnosis for children who are intensely irritable with temper tantrums but no classic mania; they do not appear to have higher bipolar risk as adults
- Self-help for bipolar disorder: know your triggers, avoid drugs and alcohol, keep a routine (such as about eight hours in bed a night), and learn from past episodes, since two nights of a small sleep change can be an early warning sign
- Early signs of mania include lack of sleep and speaking quickly; a deep depression may begin with low mood, fatigue or trouble sleeping

- Lithium's actions are specific to bipolar disorder: people without bipolar symptoms show no behavior change when given lithium
