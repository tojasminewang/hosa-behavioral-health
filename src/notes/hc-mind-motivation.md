## Emotion and motivation
- **Emotion:** arousal + physical sensations + subjective feelings that arise in response to the environment (e.g., a racing heart in fear readies us to act)
- Emotions show in facial expressions, body language, gestures, tone of voice
- **Motivation:** a process that arouses, maintains, and guides behaviour toward a goal; comes with emotional states
- **Homeostasis:** steady internal balance that motivation maintains
- **Set points:** values held steady, e.g., core body temperature, fluid levels, body weight
- Motivation starts with an internal or external stimulus cue
- **Drive:** tension and arousal from survival-related stimuli (hunger drives eating)
- **Drive reduction:** relief and reward from removing a drive

![How a drive moves us toward a goal](hc-drive)

- **Incentives:** rewards that pull behaviour; some say no drive "push" is needed
- **Incentive theories:** explain motivation without unpleasant inner drive states
- **Intrinsic reward:** comes from inside
- **Extrinsic reward:** comes from outside

## Hunger and weight
- Hunger responds to external and internal cues
- External cues: time of day, sight and smell of favourite foods, social setting
- Internal cues (real nutrient shortage): stomach contractions, low blood sugar (glucose), low stored fat
- Hunger hormones: ghrelin and orexins
- **Satiety:** feeling full, needing no more food
- **Ventromedial hypothalamus (VMH):** senses satiety from hormones and other signals
- **Leptin:** fat-cell hormone that keeps stored fat at a suitable level
- **Cholecystokinin (CCK):** digestive hormone released when food arrives

![Hunger and satiety signals](hc-hunger)

| When fat stores are… | Low (hunger) | High (satiety) |
|---|---|---|
| Hormones | Leptin low; ghrelin and orexins active | Leptin high |
| Eating | Starts | Stops |
| Autonomic nervous system | Parasympathetic: the body stores nutrients | Sympathetic: the body spends nutrients |
| Metabolic activity | Lower | Higher |
| Hypothalamus | Lateral hypothalamus drives eating | Ventromedial hypothalamus holds eating back |
| Result | Stored fat rises | Stored fat falls |

- Experts disagree about ideal weight and obesity's health impact
- **Body mass index (BMI):** common measure of body weight; under 18.5 = underweight, 18.5–24 = healthy weight, 25–29 = overweight, 30–39 = obese, over 40 = severely (morbidly) obese

![Body mass index (BMI) categories](hc-bmi)

- Obesity factors: genes (set points, metabolism; twin studies), sedentary lifestyle, inherited preference for sweet, fatty food (ancestors sought calorie-rich sugars and fats to avoid starvation), social comparison with our circles
- **Eating disorders:** unusual eating plus distorted body image (may feel obese while starving)
- **Anorexia nervosa:** unusually low body weight (food restriction, over-exercise) + distorted body image
- **Bulimia nervosa:** bingeing and purging, with depression, disgust, lost control
- **Binge-eating disorder:** huge amounts at one sitting, feeling out of control, without compensating (no vomiting or laxatives)

## Sex and orientation
- **Estrus:** hours or days when females of most mammals are receptive; hormones and sex tightly linked
- In women, ovulation and menstruation hormones barely affect sexual interest; menopause changes hormones but barely affects sexual interest
- Female sexual interest correlates with testosterone, a male hormone
- In males, testosterone within the normal range does not strongly predict sexual frequency
- Male testosterone varies through the day with biology and environment (e.g., competition)
- After competition: winners' testosterone rises, losers' drops for a while; losing at home drops it more (likely from ancestors defending territory)
- **Sexual orientation:** stable pattern of attraction to a particular sex
- Genes appear to influence orientation through complex, poorly understood mechanisms, interacting with experience, including prenatal factors

## Affiliation and achievement
- **Affiliation:** connection with other people
- Most people rate love, intimacy, and social connection above wealth, fame, even physical health
- Lacking connection can be devastating
- **Loneliness:** unpleasant signal, like hunger, thirst, pain, that evolved to warn that social connections need repair
- **Achievement motivation:** desire to excel or outperform others; resembles a personality trait
- **Trait:** stable characteristic that changes little but interacts with situations
- Trait achievement motivation interacts with environmental opportunities; fear of failure may stop capable people from trying
- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs:** the classic pyramid of motivational priorities, bottom to top: immediate physiological needs → safety → love (affection, belonging) → esteem (respect) → self-actualization
- **Updated pyramid of needs (Kenrick et al., 2010):** adds developmental and evolutionary views; bottom to top: immediate physiological needs → self-protection → affiliation → status/esteem → mate acquisition → mate retention → parenting

## Emotion and arousal
- Emotions vary in pleasure (unpleasant to pleasant) and arousal (low to high)
- On that map: unpleasant + high arousal = alarmed, afraid, tense, angry, annoyed, frustrated; pleasant + high arousal = aroused, excited, astonished, delighted; pleasant + lower arousal = happy, glad, pleased, serene, calm, relaxed; unpleasant + low arousal = miserable, depressed, sad, gloomy, bored, tired, sleepy

![The emotion map: emotions vary in pleasure and arousal](hc-emotion-map)

- **Yerkes-Dodson law:** how task complexity, arousal, and performance relate; ideal arousal depends on complexity
- Simple task (outrunning a predator): more arousal = better; complex task (difficult exam): too much arousal hurts

![The Yerkes–Dodson law (a sketch): the best amount of arousal depends on the task](hc-yerkes-dodson)

| Brain area | Role in emotion |
|---|---|
| Autonomic nervous system | General arousal; sympathetic division = fight or flight, under immediate control of the hypothalamus |
| Hypothalamus | Directly manages the autonomic nervous system; part of several emotions |
| Amygdala and insula | In the fold where the frontal and temporal lobes meet; identify emotional stimuli and start arousal |
| Cingulate cortex | Gateway between the amygdala and other subcortical structures and the frontal cortex; receives pain pathways |
| Basal ganglia | Voluntary movement; coordinate movements in response to emotional stimuli |
| Cerebral cortex | Left-hemisphere activity → positive emotions; right-hemisphere activity → negative emotions |

## Expressing and reading emotion
- Open question: are emotional expressions learned or built in?
- **Display rule:** cultural norm for when, where, and how to express an emotion
- Cultures are fairly alike in how much emotion they show, but they vary: in one comparison Switzerland held back emotion least (score 3.05) and Hong Kong most (4.72), with China, Italy, Japan and the United States in between
- Adults read emotions accurately but are worse at telling genuine from fake expressions
- **Emotional intelligence:** perceiving, using, understanding, and managing emotions; predicts work and relationship success
- Schizophrenia and autism can reduce the ability to read emotional expressions

| Theory | Sequence |
|---|---|
| James-Lange theory | Physical sensations first, leading to subjective feelings |
| Cannon-Bard theory | Physical sensations and subjective feelings at the same time |
| Schachter-Singer two-factor theory | General arousal → assessment of the situation → subjective feelings |
| Somatovisceral afference model of emotion (SAME) | Newer: sensations range from precise to general and need varying amounts of thinking before becoming feelings |
