## Biological approaches
- **Medication:** the most commonly used medical therapy
- **Electroconvulsive therapy (ECT):** some severe depression unresponsive to other treatment; anesthesia and a muscle relaxant, then electrodes on the head induce a generalized seizure
- **Psychosurgery:** brain surgery for psychological disorders
- **Brain stimulation:** implanted electrodes for some anxiety and mood disorders
- **Neurofeedback:** biofeedback for ADHD and seizures; keeping brain-activity measures in a range
- Approaches at a glance: psychoanalysis (psychodynamic theory; directive; free association, dream analysis, analysis of resistance and transference); humanistic therapy (nondirective; unconditional positive regard, reflection, empathy); behaviour therapy (behaviourism; classical and operant principles for behaviour modification, based only on observed behaviour); cognitive therapy (cognitive restructuring, combined with behaviour modification in CBT); biological treatments (medication, ECT, psychosurgery, brain stimulation, neurofeedback)

## Treating disorders
- ASD: abnormal serotonin, GABA, glutamate, but no medication proven to ease symptoms
- **Applied behaviour analysis (ABA):** most common ASD treatment; operant conditioning on observable, socially important behaviours like language; gains should last and generalize
- ADHD: medication alone or with behaviour therapy; top drugs are stimulants: methylphenidate (Ritalin), dextroamphetamine, amphetamine salts (Adderall); they boost dopamine and norepinephrine; dextroamphetamine brands include Dextrostat
- **Stimulant rationale:** impulsive hyperactivity arises when areas for planned behaviour (basal ganglia, frontal lobes) are underactive
- **Phenothiazines:** antipsychotics that block dopamine receptors (dopamine antagonists), reducing psychosis
- Nearly a quarter of patients with schizophrenia don't respond; hallucinations and delusions improve more than negative symptoms (social withdrawal, emotional disturbance)
- **Tardive dyskinesia:** tremors and involuntary movements, especially face and tongue, from phenothiazines
- Most people with schizophrenia take medication, but psychological treatment still helps
- Bipolar: medication first; most prescribed is lithium carbonate, a simple salt
- Lithium acts only on bipolar disorder: no behaviour change in people without bipolar symptoms
- Toxic lithium → nausea, vomiting, tremors, seizures, even coma, so regular blood tests are needed
- Most people with bipolar disorder need medication; psychotherapy alone isn't usually an option
- MDD: varied causes, varied treatments: medical, cognitive, behavioural, aerobic exercise, combinations
- Medication is the most popular MDD treatment today
- **Selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors (SSRIs):** most prescribed antidepressants (e.g., Prozac, Zoloft); block serotonin reuptake, leaving more in the synapse

![Where two medications act at the synapse](hc-ssri)

- Psychotherapies (notably CBT) relieve depression without adverse side effects
- Anxiety disorders (GAD, phobias, panic disorder): medication, CBT, or both
- Anxiety = heightened brain activity from expected danger, so drugs aim for a "tranquilizing" effect
- GABA is the brain's major inhibitory neurotransmitter, so boosting it slows brain activity
- GABA drugs are used occasionally; antidepressants usually work better, hinting at a shared cause of depression and anxiety

![Medications in the slides and what they do](hc-meds)

- OCD: antidepressants, CBT, or both; CBT trains anticipating a compulsion and doing a competing behaviour instead; meta-analyses show it is highly effective
- Body dysmorphic disorder: CBT helps, with cognitive restructuring for realistic thinking about perceived flaws; many keep seeking cosmetic surgery and resist psychotherapy
- PTSD: exposure therapy, with or without SSRIs; co-occurring substance use disorders also need treatment
- Dissociative identity disorder: diagnosis and treatment are controversial; some therapists see it as a valid diagnosis often following serious trauma (especially childhood sexual abuse) and work through traumatic memories to integrate personalities
- Hypnosis for recovering memories is not well advised: suggestible people may create false memories, and people with this diagnosis are far more suggestible than average
- Somatic symptom disorders (e.g., pain without physical basis): biological and psychological treatment; best biological ones are antidepressants and more physical activity
- ASPD: high priority given the harm caused, but effective treatment is elusive; no medication targets antisocial behaviour, though inmates may get antipsychotics and anticonvulsants to manage aggression
- People with ASPD tend to see themselves as "okay", not needing treatment
- ASPD psychological treatment: learning models for anger control, social skills, moral reasoning
- BPD: no approved medication, but antidepressants, antipsychotics, mood stabilizers (e.g., lithium), anti-anxiety drugs, and anticonvulsants are often prescribed
- BPD psychotherapy is often CBT: managing stress, emotions, relationships, and especially suicide risk
- **Dialectical behaviour therapy (DBT):** designed for BPD; treats in order: suicidal thoughts, then therapy-interfering behaviours, then quality-of-life behaviours

## Recovery
- Recovery has four major dimensions: health, home, purpose, and community

| Dimension | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Health | Overcoming or managing disease through informed, healthy choices (e.g., abstaining from alcohol, illicit drugs, non-prescribed medication) |
| Home | A stable, safe place to live |
| Purpose | Meaningful daily activities for independence, income, and taking part in society (job, volunteering, caregiving, creative work) |
| Community | Relationships giving support, friendship, love, and hope |
