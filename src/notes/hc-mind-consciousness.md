## Consciousness
- **Consciousness:** awareness of experiences inside us and around us
- **Self-awareness:** understanding the self as separate from other stimuli
- Thalamus and reticular formation: may be necessary but not sufficient
- Better seen as changing activity in brain networks than as particular structures
- **Default mode network (DMN):** keeps up unconscious background activity; readies the brain for conscious thought
- Varying alertness may help repair the body and save energy (being awake 24 hours would need more food)
- Safety: animals are built for light or dark, so sleeping through the other part may aid survival
- Awareness lets animals choose responses instead of reflex and instinct (hearing a possible predator: keep going, flee, or pause for more information), likely improving survival

## Sleep and daily rhythms
- **Sleep:** normal state with reduced awareness of outside stimuli
- **Waking:** normal state of alertness and awareness of outside stimuli
- **Biological clock:** internal mechanism setting a rough schedule for body processes
- **Circadian rhythm:** daily biological rhythm; controlled by the hypothalamus; responds to internal and external factors
- Disruption → jet lag; too much change → slower reaction time, minor memory loss; rhythms differ (morning people, night owls)
- Eastward travel is harder: an LA resident in New York sleeps at what feels like 7:00 p.m. and wakes at what feels like 3:00 a.m.; a New Yorker in LA just stays up later (1:00 a.m. NY time) and sleeps in (9:00 a.m. NY time)

![Why flying east is harder than flying west](hc-jet-lag)

- **Major depressive disorder with seasonal pattern:** depression at the same time yearly, usually winter, when less light sets the internal clock; ~4%–6% of people; treated with bright light, with or without antidepressants

## Brain waves (EEG)
- **Electroencephalogram (EEG):** recording of overall brain activity used to describe states of awareness
- Awake: gamma, beta, alpha; N-REM sleep: theta, delta

| Wave | Speed | Usually means |
|---|---|---|
| Gamma waves | More than 30 cycles per second | Attention to sensory input |
| Beta waves | 15 to 30 cycles per second | Alert waking |
| Alpha waves | Slower than beta | Relaxed waking |
| Theta waves | 4 to 7 cycles per second | The lighter stages of N-REM sleep |
| Delta waves | 1 to 4 cycles per second | Deep N-REM sleep |

![An EEG during sleep. The screen shows the five kinds of brain waves, fastest at the top and slowest at the bottom.](hc-sleep-lab)

- **Daydreaming:** spontaneous experiences with no task, stimulus, or response; linked to the DMN; purpose unclear

## N-REM and REM sleep
- **Non-rapid eye movement (N-REM) sleep:** theta and delta waves, deep physical relaxation; four stages
- Stage 1: falling asleep; so brief people deny sleeping; theta waves
- Stage 2: heart rate and muscle tension drop; K complexes and sleep spindles; sleep through familiar stimuli, wake to unexpected ones
- Stages 3–4: deep sleep with delta waves (more in stage 4, otherwise similar); very disoriented if woken
- **Rapid eye movement (REM) sleep:** waking-like EEG, rapid eye movements, muscle paralysis, sympathetic activation
- REM starts ~90 to 120 minutes after falling asleep
- **Paradoxical sleep:** REM's other name: brain looks awake, body looks deeply asleep
- In REM: very active sympathetic system; rapid, irregular heart rate, blood pressure, breathing; eyes dart; muscles twitch
- REM paralysis stops us acting out dreams and getting hurt, and prevents sleepwalking
- Sleepwalking: stages 3–4 of N-REM; sleep talking: stages 1–2
- A night's sleep mixes N-REM and REM

![The stages of sleep](hc-sleep-stages)

- Only birds and mammals show REM, suggesting it evolved fairly recently

## Why we sleep and dream
- Stages 3–4 of N-REM sleep repair the body and release growth hormone
- All sleep restores energy and improves memory
- REM loss → irritability, trouble concentrating
- **REM rebound:** after REM deprivation, sleep is mostly REM
- REM and mood are linked: depression can bring more REM; antidepressants suppress REM
- Serotonin and norepinephrine neurons: quiet in REM, somewhat active in N-REM
- **Dreaming:** mental state in sleep with visual imagery; content reflects brain activity (e.g., dreaming you can't move reflects REM paralysis)
- **Lucid dreaming:** knowing you are dreaming and controlling the dream; more dorsolateral prefrontal cortex activity (sense of control, voluntary behaviour)

## Sleep disorders
- **Sleep terrors:** sudden waking from N-REM in great distress without nightmare imagery; disoriented and confused if woken
- **Nightmares:** often confused with sleep terrors but occur in REM
- **Insomnia:** can't start or maintain normal sleep; most common sleep problem after nightmares
- **Onset insomnia:** lying in bed a long time unable to fall asleep
- **Maintenance insomnia:** interrupted sleep or early waking; usually stress
- Zolpidem (insomnia drug): very small effects on sleep
- **Narcolepsy:** "sleep attacks" of REM during waking, plus sleep or muscle paralysis; orexins missing or damaged (genes + autoimmune attack on orexin-making cells)
- **Cataplexy:** REM paralysis while awake, without losing consciousness; touching the person ends it
- **Sleep apnea:** breathing stops for a minute or two; sleeper wakes gasping; mostly in obese snorers (treat with weight loss, airway surgery); raises heart disease risk; can stem from faulty brainstem breathing neurons
- **Sudden infant death syndrome (SIDS):** a healthy infant dies in sleep; cause unclear; top cause of death in the first six months in industrialized nations; rates fell with back-sleeping instead of stomach-sleeping
- **Restless legs syndrome (RLS):** involuntary movement of a limb, usually one leg, tingling and moving at regular intervals; gene variant active in the basal ganglia early in prenatal development

## Brain damage and consciousness
- Both the location and the severity of damage change consciousness
- **Coma:** deep unconsciousness: no sleep–wake cycles, can't be woken, no response to pain or light, no voluntary behaviour
- Coma follows damage to both hemispheres or the reticular formation; within 2–4 weeks most recover, enter a vegetative state, or die; EEGs differ by cause
- **Vegetative state (VS):** wakefulness without consciousness after brain injury, often after coma; eyes open at times; may grind teeth, scream, smile, cry; some respond to pain; awareness still debated despite imaging
- **Brain death:** complete, irreversible loss of measurable brain activity: two flat EEGs 24 hours apart or no blood flow to the brain; no response to any stimulus including pain; no cranial-nerve reflexes
- **Near-death experience:** altered state near death: "out of body", light at the end of a tunnel, calm, reluctant return
- Animals near death show heightened consciousness; a damaged brain may release chemicals that limit cell loss

## Seizures
- **Seizure:** sudden abnormal brain activation; changes in consciousness show cortical activity is tied to consciousness
- **Epilepsy:** diagnosis for recurring seizures
- Causes: brain injury, infection, or no obvious cause
- **Partial seizure:** starts in one area (the focal area); often has an aura; temporal-lobe seizures can cause déjà vu
- **Aura:** sense that a seizure is coming
- **Generalized seizure:** abnormal activation of cortex–thalamus circuits; no single starting area; no aura
- **Tonic-clonic seizure:** loss of consciousness, stopped breathing, intense contraction, violent rhythmic jerking (possible injuries), then a short coma
- **Absence seizure:** loss of consciousness; movement limited to blinking, head turns, eye movements

## Psychoactive drugs
- **Psychoactive drug:** any drug that can change consciousness
- **Hallucinogen:** drug producing false perceptions
- **Stimulant:** drug increasing nervous system activity
- **Tolerance:** needing more drug for the same effect, as the nervous system compensates
- **Withdrawal:** physical reactions to stopping a habitual drug
- **Addiction:** compulsive physical or psychological dependence on a substance or activity despite negative consequences; short-term outcomes win over long-term ones
- Addiction involves three systems: impulsive (dopamine pathways, midbrain–basal ganglia), reflective (frontal lobes weigh pros and cons, control impulses), craving (insula)

![Three systems involved in addiction](hc-addiction)

| Drug | Acts on | Effects |
|---|---|---|
| Marijuana (cannabis) | Endogenous cannabinoids | Excitement, vivid imagery, mild euphoria (or depression, withdrawal); distorts time and space; some hallucinate, so it is classed as a hallucinogen |
| LSD | Serotonin | Vivid colourful hallucinations, intrusive hallucinations, flashbacks |
| Caffeine | Adenosine | Alertness; withdrawal brings fatigue and headaches |
| Nicotine | Acetylcholine | Tobacco raises heart rate and blood pressure, reduces fatigue, sharpens thinking, relaxes muscles |
| Cocaine and amphetamines | Dopamine | Alertness, good mood, confidence; hallucinations, delusional fears of harm, repetitive movements (chewing, teeth grinding) |
| Methylphenidate (Ritalin) | Dopamine and norepinephrine | Better alertness and concentration; affects sleep and appetite |
| MDMA (ecstasy) | Serotonin and oxytocin | Raises heart rate, blood pressure, body temperature; excess serotonin → dehydration, exhaustion, hyperthermia, convulsions, death |
| Alcohol | GABA and dopamine | Low doses widen blood vessels (warm, flushed) and lower anxiety; high doses → aggression, risk-taking, poor coordination; very high → coma or death (suppressed breathing, inhaled vomit) |
| Opioids | Endogenous endorphins | Big dopamine release → well-being, pain relief; low doses: euphoria, calm, relaxation, sleep; high doses: a strong "rush" and depressed breathing, sometimes fatal |

## Without drugs
- **Hypnosis:** altered state with relaxation and greater suggestibility
- **Meditation:** voluntary change in consciousness with positive emotion and no thought
- Others: high altitude, scuba diving, starvation, sleep deprivation, sensory deprivation, twirling, rhythm-induced trance
