## Why prevention matters (SAMHSA)
- Prevention activities educate and support individuals and communities to prevent the use and misuse of drugs and the development of substance use disorders
- Mental and substance use disorders are among the top conditions that cause disability in the United States; preventing mental, substance use, and co-occurring disorders is critical to behavioral and physical health
- Prevention and early intervention strategies can reduce the impact of substance use and mental disorders in America's communities
- SAMHSA's **Center for Substance Abuse Prevention (CSAP)** provides national leadership in policies, programs, and services to prevent the onset of substance misuse
- The **Evidence-Based Practices Resource Center** gives communities, clinicians, and policymakers information to use evidence-based practices for prevention, treatment, and recovery
- Prevention of mental, emotional, and behavioral disorders focuses on addressing known risk factors, such as exposure to trauma
- When young people understand the risks of drug use, they are less likely to start; parents, teachers, and health care providers play a critical role in educating youth

## The Strategic Prevention Framework (SAMHSA)
- **Strategic Prevention Framework (SPF):** SAMHSA's comprehensive approach (five steps and two guiding principles) for understanding substance use problems in their community context and developing and sustaining effective prevention programs

| SPF step | What it means | Key question |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Assessment | Identify local prevention needs based on data | What is the problem? |
| 2. Capacity | Build local resources and readiness to address prevention needs | What do you have to work with? |
| 3. Planning | Find out what works to address prevention needs and how to do it | What should you do and how should you do it? |
| 4. Implementation | Deliver evidence-based programs and practices as intended | How can you put your plan into action? |
| 5. Evaluation | Examine the process and outcomes of programs and practices | Is your plan succeeding? |

![The Strategic Prevention Framework is a cycle, not a straight line](spf-cycle)

- Research shows prevention must begin with an understanding of behavioral health problems within their environmental contexts before communities implement solutions
- SPF defining characteristics: **dynamic and iterative** (a circular rather than linear model; assessment is the starting point but practitioners return to it repeatedly), **data-informed** (data guide all prevention decisions), and reliant on a **team approach** (community partners in every step)
- Communities receiving SAMHSA prevention funding are expected to use the SPF to guide prevention planning
- The **Strategic Prevention Technical Assistance Center (SPTAC)** is SAMHSA's national training and technical assistance system that uses the SPF as its roadmap

## The six primary prevention strategies (SAMHSA SUBG)
| Strategy | Description |
|---|---|
| Information dissemination | Increases knowledge and awareness of alcohol and drug use, addiction, and their effects, and of available services; one-way communication with limited contact between source and audience |
| Education | Builds skills through structured learning; critical life and social skills include decision making, peer resistance, coping with stress, problem solving, interpersonal communication, and systematic and judgmental capabilities; more interaction than information dissemination |
| Alternatives | Provide healthy activities that exclude alcohol and other drugs to discourage use |
| Problem identification and referral | Identifies people who have used tobacco or alcohol illegally or age-inappropriately or first used illicit drugs, to assess whether their behavior can be reversed through education; does not include determining whether a person needs treatment |
| Community-based process | Ongoing networking and technical assistance to community groups or agencies; neighborhood-based, grassroots empowerment models using action planning and collaborative systems planning |
| Environmental | Establishes or changes written and unwritten community standards, codes, and attitudes to influence the general population's use of alcohol and other drugs |

- **Primary prevention:** Strategies directed at individuals not identified to be in need of treatment; SUBG grantees must spend no less than 20% of their allotment on substance use primary prevention
- A comprehensive primary prevention program must target both the general population and sub-groups at high risk

## The IOM prevention model (SAMHSA)
| IOM category | Target population |
|---|---|
| Universal | The general public or a whole population group not identified on the basis of individual risk |
| Selective | Individuals or a subgroup whose risk of developing a disorder is significantly higher than average |
| Indicated | Individuals in high-risk environments with minimal but detectable signs or symptoms foreshadowing a disorder, or biological markers of predisposition, who do not yet meet diagnostic levels |

![The IOM model: universal, selective and indicated prevention](iom-model)

- The **Institute of Medicine (IOM) Model** classifies preventive interventions by targeted population: universal, selective, and indicated
- Block grants fund primary prevention through universal, selective, and indicated prevention activities for persons not identified as needing treatment

## Prevention campaigns and programs (SAMHSA)
- **"Talk. They Hear You." campaign:** SAMHSA's national youth substance use prevention campaign that aims to reduce underage drinking and other substance use among youths under 21 by giving parents and caregivers information to talk with their children early and often; it celebrated its 10th anniversary in 2023
- Parents and caregivers have a significant influence on their children's decisions about using alcohol and other drugs
- **Screen4Success:** A 10-minute screening on the free "Talk. They Hear You." app to look for signs of risk in yourself, your child, or someone you care about
- **Interagency Coordinating Committee on the Prevention of Underage Drinking (ICCPUD):** Led by SAMHSA and made up of 15 federal agencies; coordinates federal efforts to reduce underage drinking
- **STOP Underage Drinking:** ICCPUD's interagency website portal of research, resources, and funding
- **National Prevention Week (NPW):** Held in May; a public education platform showcasing community work to prevent substance use and promote well-being
- **Communities Talk to Prevent Alcohol and Other Drug Misuse:** A nationwide initiative (since 2006) giving community organizations, colleges, and state organizations resources and planning stipends to educate youth and young adults ages 12 to 25; youth-led events are among the most impactful
- **Prevention Technology Transfer Center (PTTC) Network:** Training and technical assistance for the prevention field through 10 regional centers
- **Project LAUNCH:** Promotes the wellness of young children from birth to 8 years of age (social, emotional, cognitive, physical, and behavioral development)
- **Infant and Early Childhood Mental Health (IECMH) Grant Program:** Improves outcomes for children from birth up to 12 years of age
- **Project AWARE (Advancing Wellness and Resiliency in Education):** Increases mental health awareness in schools across states, territories, and tribal communities
- **TISS:** School-Based Trauma-Informed Support Services and Mental Health Care for Children and Youth; increases student access to trauma support services
- Schools and campuses face public health issues such as bullying, violence, alcohol use, and drug abuse; bullying and peer pressure can begin as early as preschool
- Of 8.9 million young adults with a mental illness in 2018, more than 2 in 5 went untreated, and of 5.1 million with a substance use disorder, nearly 9 in 10 did not get treatment
- Family support can play a major role in helping a loved one with mental and substance use disorders; growing up in a home affected by a family member's mental illness or substance use raises risk; support groups and family therapy improve treatment effectiveness

## Suicide data and risk factors (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: About Suicide](https://www.samhsa.gov/mental-health/suicidal-behavior/about)
- Suicide is complex and determined by multiple factors such as mental illness, substance misuse, chronic illness, trauma, painful losses, exposure to violence, and social isolation
- In 2024, suicide was a leading cause of death in the U.S. for people aged 10 and up; 48,824 people died by suicide in 2024
- Suicides outnumbered homicides in the U.S. from 2014 to 2023
- In 2025, 13.9 million adults reported seriously considering suicide and 2.3 million reported a suicide attempt
- In 2024, suicide rates ranged from 2.31 per 100,000 among youth ages 10–14 to 21.4 per 100,000 among people 85 and older
- Risk and protective factors occur at multiple levels: individual, relationship, community, and societal

| Level | Risk factors | Protective factors |
|---|---|---|
| Individual | Untreated mental illness and substance misuse | Problem-solving and coping skills, positive self-esteem, a sense of purpose in life |
| Relationship | Family conflict, knowing someone who died by suicide (especially a family member), social isolation | Family connectedness, supportive friendships |
| Community | Community violence, historical trauma, access to lethal means | Access to behavioral health services, cultural beliefs that discourage suicide, connectedness to community institutions |
| Societal | Stigma, racism, discrimination | Cultural identification, policies such as anti-discrimination laws |

## Preventing suicide (SAMHSA)
- Suicide is a significant public health problem, but it is not inevitable; everyone has a role in prevention
- Faith communities can help people find hope, meaning, and purpose and encourage behavioral health care
- Schools can offer suicide prevention programs with early intervention and staff training, and employee assistance programs
- Local health care organizations can integrate routine mental health screening into primary care visits and provide follow-up after a crisis
- Community centers can host support groups with wellness activities; businesses can promote mental health awareness
- **Effective Suicide Prevention Model (SPRC):** A flexible framework emphasizing strategic planning, keys to success, and a comprehensive approach
- **Best Practices Registry (BPR):** The Suicide Prevention Resource Center's list of expert-reviewed, effective prevention programs
- **Zero Suicide Framework:** Built on the belief that suicide can be prevented within care systems by improving access, quality, and support, with a strong commitment and a culture where suicide is no longer acceptable
- SAMHSA's suicide prevention initiatives include the Black Youth Suicide Prevention Initiative; the National Strategy for Suicide Prevention and Federal Action Plan guide national efforts

## Drug-free workplaces (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Drug-Free Workplace](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/drug-free-workplace/about)
- SAMHSA's **Division of Workplace Programs (DWP)** oversees programs to eliminate illicit drug use in federal workplaces and helps all workplaces become drug-free
- DWP oversees federal drug-free workplace programs and the **National Laboratory Certification Program** (certifies labs for forensic drug testing for federal agencies and federally regulated industries)
- The Federal Drug-Free Workplace Program certifies executive branch agencies' plans and identifies safety-sensitive positions subject to random drug testing
- The **Drug Testing Advisory Board (DTAB)** advises SAMHSA on drug testing and lab certification
- **Drug-Free Workplace Helpline:** 1-800-WORKPLACE (967-5752); free, confidential help for employees and families, Monday through Friday

## Garrett Lee Smith suicide prevention grants (SAMHSA)
@ [SAMHSA: Substance Use Disorder Prevention](https://www.samhsa.gov/substance-use/prevention/substance-use-disorders)
- **Garrett Lee Smith (GLS) Campus Suicide Prevention Grant:** Builds a coordinated approach to behavioral health services for all college students, to prevent mental and substance use disorders, promote help-seeking, and identify and treat students at risk for suicide
- **Garrett Lee Smith (GLS) State/Tribal Youth Suicide Prevention and Early Intervention Grant Program:** Supports states and tribes with youth suicide prevention and early intervention in schools, youth-serving organizations and health care settings
